Buying a US stock from India costs more in the currency than in the trade

A teardown of how Indian residents invest in US stocks on INDmoney, Vested and Groww: the rules they operate under, how money actually moves, what it costs, and what a new entrant should build first.

The short version

Every platform advertises low or zero fees, and the brokerage really is small at about 0.25% a trade. The bigger cost is the rupee to dollar conversion, which banks price at roughly 0.5% to 1.2% each way. On a simple round trip, the FX spread makes up about 80% of what the investor loses.

The second finding is that this is a money movement business more than a trading business. Groww shut its first US stocks product in 2024 not because trading was hard, but because remittances, withdrawal fees and settlement delays broke the experience. INDmoney and Vested grew by owning that plumbing.

The timing matters. Since the GIFT City Global Access Provider (GAP) framework opened in August 2025, the big domestic brokers have received approval to offer US stocks. Upstox launched in September 2026, and Groww was testing a relaunch as of mid-2026. The incumbents' lead is now in funding speed, cost transparency and tax tooling, not access.

The rules every platform works within

No app can change these. They shape the product, the funnel and the support queue.

$250,000 a yearLiberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) cap per resident, across all purposes: travel, education, gifts and investing combined.
Nil TCS to ₹10 lakhNo tax collected at source on the first ₹10 lakh of total LRS remittances in a financial year. The threshold rose from ₹7 lakh in Budget 2025.
20% TCS above thatFor investments, 20% is collected upfront on the excess. It is refundable against income tax, but the cash is locked until the return is filed. Budget 2026 cut rates for education and medical, not investing.
25% on dividendsUS withholding on dividends drops from 30% to 25% once a W-8BEN form is filed, claimable as a foreign tax credit in India.
24 months for long termGains on foreign shares held 24 months or more are taxed at 12.5%. Shorter holdings are taxed at slab rates.
Schedule FA every yearForeign holdings must be declared in the income tax return, which makes clean tax reports a retention feature, not a nice-to-have.

A concrete TCS example: someone who invests ₹15 lakh in US stocks in FY 2026-27, with no other remittances, has ₹1,00,000 collected upfront (20% of the ₹5 lakh above the threshold). The limit is cumulative across all banks, so a platform cannot see the full picture on its own.

The investor journey, step by step

Mapped from each platform's published flows. Amber notes mark where users drop off or raise tickets.

  1. Open an account and complete KYCPAN and Aadhaar based, fully digital on all three. INDmoney files the W-8BEN on the user's behalf. A second KYC just for US stocks is a drop-off point for existing domestic users.
  2. Declare the remittance under LRSEvery transfer needs a Form A2 declaration and bank compliance checks. INDmoney handles A2 inside the app. On the older route, users go to their bank portal with SWIFT details. Income or source-of-funds document requests surprise first-time investors.
  3. Convert rupees to dollarsThe bank applies its spread. Apps show a rate, but rarely the total dollars lost versus the reference rate. The largest cost in the journey, and the least visible.
  4. Wait for the wallet to be creditedINDmoney's GIFT City wallet is typically credited within hours via partner banks. A classic SWIFT transfer takes 2 to 3 business days. "Where is my money" is the top support theme for this category.
  5. Buy, including fractionsFractional investing from $1 on all active platforms, which matters when one share of a large US company costs more than a month's SIP. US market hours are late evening to early morning in India.
  6. Receive dividendsCredited in USD after 25% withholding. Small fractional dividends sit idle unless the app offers reinvestment.
  7. Sell and bring money homeBrokerage again, FX spread again, and on some platforms a withdrawal fee. This step is what ended Groww's first product.
  8. File taxesSchedule FA, capital gains and Form 67 for the foreign tax credit. Both INDmoney and Vested generate reports, which keeps users from leaving at tax time.

Platform by platform

INDmoney

Won by turning remittance into a feature.

INDmoney runs US stocks through its own IFSCA-licensed entity in GIFT City and has been an authorised Global Access Provider since August 2025. Shares are held with DriveWealth and Alpaca in the US. The key product decision is the funding flow: direct integrations with HDFC, ICICI, Kotak, Federal and IDFC, Form A2 filed in-app, live tracking, and money typically in the wallet within hours rather than days.

Pricing is 0.25% brokerage capped at $25 per order, with no account, maintenance, platform or withdrawal fee. The bank's FX spread of 0.5% to 1.2% is disclosed separately. It also takes over users from other apps, such as Fi's US stocks customers in 2026, and claims more than 3 million US stock accounts.

Gap: US stocks sit inside a super-app with Indian stocks, mutual funds and loans, so discovery depends on the user already knowing they want global exposure.

Vested

The specialist that sells breadth and guidance.

Vested is built only for global investing. Its US broker-dealer, VF Securities, is a FINRA member with custody through DriveWealth, and Vested also holds IFSCA GAP and broker-dealer authorisation at GIFT City. Beyond US stocks and ETFs, it offers managed thematic portfolios, 350+ global funds, pre-IPO private market deals from $10,000, and RSU/ESOP transfers.

It uses a freemium model: Basic has 0.25% brokerage, Premium (₹375 a month billed annually) cuts it to 0.15%, both capped at $35. INR withdrawals above $100 are free, smaller ones cost $3, and USD withdrawals cost $5. Funding runs through partner banks such as HDFC, ICICI and Axis, or a guided transfer from any bank. As of June 2026 it reported 4 lakh+ investors and ₹10,000 crore+ in assets.

Gap: the paid plan only pays back for high-turnover users, and the product assumes some investing confidence. That suits its affluent, RSU-holding audience but limits mass reach.

Groww

A cautionary tale, now attempting a comeback.

Groww launched US stocks in 2020 through a US broker partner. It stopped onboarding new users around early 2023, halted new purchases and wallet top-ups from 27 February 2024, and moved existing holders out. In its own words, remittance processes, high withdrawal fees and settlement delays created a broken experience it could not fix. Reports at the time put active US stock investors at only about 2,000, tiny against its domestic base.

In June 2026 Groww received IFSCA approval to offer international investing through GIFT City, and in July management said the product was in testing, starting with US stocks. The GIFT City route directly addresses the reasons it quit: domestic-style funding and no withdrawal charges when bringing funds home.

Lesson: distribution alone does not make this category work. A 13 million active-user broker could not make US stocks stick while money movement was slow and expensive.

Put differently, a long-term investor needs about a 2.5% gain just to break even on a single entry and exit. The FX spread is roughly four times the brokerage, yet brokerage is the number most marketing focuses on.

What I would build first as a new entrant

Access is now a commodity: every large broker can list 8,000+ US stocks from $1. These three bets target the frictions that decided winners and losers so far.

Show dollars received, not just a rate

Before a user confirms a transfer, show one line: "₹1,00,000 becomes $1,042.21. That is ₹990 less than the market rate." Add the expected cost to bring it back. It turns the least visible cost into a trust signal and makes a tighter spread a selling point the user can see.

Measure: transfer confirmation rate, FX-related support tickets, repeat funding within 30 days.

A TCS headroom meter

Track the user's platform remittances against the ₹10 lakh threshold, let them add remittances made elsewhere in the year, and warn before a transfer crosses the line, with the exact TCS amount and when it can be claimed back. No platform can see other banks, so self-declaration is the practical first version.

Measure: surprise-TCS tickets, share of users who add outside remittances, large-ticket transfer completion.

Treat withdrawal as a launch-critical flow

Groww's exit list reads like a withdrawal backlog. Publish a withdrawal time promise, show live tracking back to the Indian bank, and make small withdrawals free. For a broker with an existing domestic base, add a one-tap "move to Indian account" from the US wallet so money never feels trapped.

Measure: median time from sell to INR credited, withdrawal ticket rate, 90-day retention of users who have withdrawn once.

Open questions I would validate

Which matters more to first-time US investors: speed of funding or visible cost? Does fractional investing pull in genuinely new users or just smaller tickets from existing ones? How much of the TCS problem disappears for users investing under ₹10 lakh a year, which is likely most retail users? These are the first things I would test with user interviews and funnel data.
